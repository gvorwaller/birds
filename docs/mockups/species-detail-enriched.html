Finding this bird

Browse field guide →

Look for Marbled Godwits actively foraging on expansive intertidal mudflats and sandy ocean beaches, most reliably during a falling tide and through low tide as freshly exposed wet substrate uncovers benthic invertebrates. Birds probe deeply with their long, slightly upturned, bicolored bills, often feeding in compact flocks alongside Willets and Short-billed Dowitchers. At high tide they typically gather into dense, resting communal roosts on upper beaches or coastal salt pannes.

About Marbled Godwit

Browse field guide →
IUCN Status: LC · Least Concern
Mass: 280–510 g
Wingspan: 70–82 cm
Length: 42–48 cm
The marbled godwit (Limosa fedoa) is a large shorebird in the sandpiper family, Scolopacidae. It is one of the four species of godwits, characterized by its long legs and very long, bicolored bill with a slight upward curve. Adults have mottled brown and buff plumage with distinctive cinnamon wing linings visible in flight. It breeds in the northern prairies of North America and winters along the Atlantic, Pacific, and Gulf coasts of the United States and Mexico. They feed by probing deeply into wet mud, sand, and shallow water for aquatic invertebrates, mollusks, worms, and crustaceans. Their populations declined drastically in the 19th century due to unregulated hunting and extensive loss of native prairie breeding habitat, but numbers have stabilized under modern conservation protections.
Description ›

Adults have long blue-grey legs and a very long, slightly upturned pink bill with a dark tip. The neck, breast, and belly are pale brown with dark brown barring on the breast and flanks. The back is mottled dark brown and tawny. They show bright cinnamon wing linings in flight, which distinguishes them from other large shorebirds like the Long-billed Curlew.

Habitat ›

During the breeding season, Marbled Godwits nest in native shortgrass and mixed-grass prairie marshes and wetland edges in the northern Great Plains. On migration and during winter, they occupy coastal environments including tidal mudflats, sandy ocean beaches, salt marshes, and estuaries.

Behaviour ›

They are gregarious outside the nesting season, often roosting and foraging in sizable flocks. When resting during high tide, flocks gather on sandbars or upper beaches, standing tightly packed with bills tucked under their scapular feathers.

Feeding ›

These birds forage by walking steadily through wet sand or shallow water, repeatedly probing deep into the mud with their sensitive bill tips. Prey items include polychaete worms, bivalves, crabs, amphipods, and aquatic insect larvae. In inland prairie habitats, they also consume grasshoppers and seeds.

Breeding ›

Nesting occurs on the ground in open prairie grasslands, usually near wetland sloughs. The nest is a shallow scrape lined with dry grasses. A typical clutch consists of four olive-buff eggs marked with darker brown spots, incubated by both parents for roughly 24 to 26 days.

Migration ›

Marbled Godwits migrate south in late summer to coastal wintering grounds extending along both coasts of North America southward to Central America. Large staging concentrations occur at major coastal estuaries such as Humboldt Bay and the Gulf of California.
