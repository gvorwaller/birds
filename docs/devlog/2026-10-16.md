## 14:05 EDT - Parts of a Bird in flight (td-a1bfd9)

### Summary
The owner asked to see each bird in flight, "to better see the feathers/wings/tail separation and placement". All four birds on Parts of a Bird are now also drawn with their wings spread, from above and from below. A selected part stays selected when switching views, so the same feathers can be found on the open wing. Deployed as cb16d32.

### What Was Done

**Owner decisions**
- Views: from above **and** from below, eight new drawings in total.
- Layout: a **Standing / Flight, above / Flight, below** switch, one drawing at a time.
- Keep the **Whole bird / One wing, larger** size toggle. I proposed dropping it after the owner said the whole bird was tappable on a phone, and the owner corrected me.
- Tapping a part's name in the list is as instructive as tapping the drawing, so the list stays a first-class control.

**Drawings**
- Process: a gull pilot first, then all four birds.
- Both owner checkpoints were tappable Artifact previews:
  - pilot: "very workable";
  - all four birds: "I hate to ever call something perfect, but imo this is it."
- Pose: symmetric, wings spread, head up the page. Only the right half is drawn, then mirrored, so each part is one id drawn on both wings.
- A shared `Wing` helper, `scripts/bird-parts/flight.py`, splits each wing into its feather rows. Each row is a band of the chord, measured from the trailing edge:
  - arm: secondaries, then the greater, median and lesser coverts, with tertials at the root;
  - hand: primaries, primary coverts and alula;
  - underside: wing linings (underwing coverts) and axillaries;
  - also: the trailing edge, the dark wingtip, and lines between individual flight feathers.
- The gull pilot was traced from CC BY-SA Commons flight photos, used for proportions only and cited in the gull's sources. The other three were drawn from known proportions.
- Each bird's flight drawings:
  - **Song Sparrow:** a rounded wing, wing bars, a pale supercilium on the crown, and a fanned tail with its rectrices.
  - **Lesser Yellowlegs:** long, pointed dark wings with no wing stripe, white uppertail coverts behind a dark rump, a barred tail, and trailing yellow legs.
  - **Mallard drake:** a blue speculum with a white border on each side (greater-covert tips in front, secondary tips behind), the curled tail feathers, a chestnut breast and orange feet.
  - **Herring Gull:** a black wingtip with mirrors and apical spots, and a white trailing edge.
- `common.build()` gained:
  - part `marks`: areas in another tone drawn over a part, which never catch taps, e.g. the gull's black wingtip over the primaries;
  - `wing_view_box`: the frame for "One wing, larger".
- The four standing JSON files are byte-identical after regenerating.

**App**

  - `placePart` keeps the current view if it draws the part, otherwise uses the first view that does.
  - Also new: `layerAfterViewChange` and `viewsDrawing`.
- Deep links to flight-only parts work, e.g. `#gull-axillaries` opens the view from below.

  - the caption says "Not drawn in this view. Show it:" with buttons, and moves focus to the part's name after a switch;
  - the size toggle.
- `PartsDiagram.svelte`, drawing:
  - the selection is repainted on top, filled and dashed, because feather rows overlap;
  - without JS, all three drawings show as labelled figures, with the full glossary.
- On phones (<640px), each switch is one row of equal columns.

  - **shorebird and gull:** rump and uppertail coverts;

  - **gull:** primary coverts, alula and the white trailing edge;
  - **duck:** wing coverts (forewing), the lesser and median coverts, primary coverts, alula, secondaries, belly and legs.

### Key Technical Insight
- **Overlapping parts need a deliberate paint order.** The standing drawings partition the silhouette, so no two parts overlap. In the flight drawings, rows and marks overlap on purpose (the trailing edge over the secondaries, mirrors over the primaries). Draw order then decides both what is visible and what a tap hits.
  - The bug CODEX1 found was a later, larger part covering part of an earlier one.
  - Check it with an elementFromPoint hit-test at a known point, not only by looking at the drawing.
  - The selected part is repainted on top, so a highlight is never buried under another row.
- **No new breakpoints:** cs.md allows only 640px and 1024px. A route test now forbids any other width media query in `PartsDiagram`.
- **A "Show it" button that switches views removes itself.** Once the part is drawn, the button is gone, so focus must move to the part's name in the list.
- **Size:** the page chunk is 232KB raw, about 62KB gzipped, and loads only on `/parts-of-a-bird`.

## 16:25 EDT - Part names on species pages link to Parts of a Bird (td-e4c6dd)

### Summary
Technical part names on species pages now link to that part, highlighted, on Parts of a Bird. Examples: supercilium, tertials, wing bars, speculum, eye ring. Deployed as 3d72706. These are the first deep links into Parts of a Bird in production.

### What Was Done

**Owner decisions**
- Where: Similar species notes, and the Wikipedia sections titled exactly "Description" or "Description and taxonomy". Not the opening paragraph, not "Finding this bird".
- Words: technical terms only. No crown, nape, rump, flanks, mantle, back, breast, belly, throat, bill, tail, wing or legs.
- If a part is missing from the bird's own drawing, link to a drawing that has it.
- Link only the first mention per note or section.
- Plain links in the text (the WCAG inline-link exception), not 48px tap targets.

**Build**
- `src/lib/bird-parts/terms.ts`, pure with no geometry imports:
  - family → drawing (ducks, gulls and skuas, shorebird families, else songbird);
  - 31 terms with their spellings;
  - one combined regex;
  - a hand-checked start boundary, because lookbehind breaks older Safari.
- `PartTermText.svelte` renders text and anchors only, never `{@html}`. It uses the app's path trail, so "Back to …" reopens the section and refocuses the link.
- Parts page path-node fix:
  - The node now registers pathname + hash.
  - A new `+page.ts` reads `returnTo`/`returnLabel`, so the back link names the species without JS.
- Plan: `docs/2026-10-16-part-term-links-plan-CC.md`. CODEX1 plan review: four P2s, all integrated. CODEX1 code review: no findings.

**Verification**
- Prod audit:
  - 5,861 links over 25,807 notes;
  - 9,960 links over 7,541 Description sections;
  - 100 random links hand-checked, 0 wrong.
- Playwright on Chromium and WebKit, 5 themes, at 320, 390 and 1200px.
- iOS Simulator: the Mallard "speculum" link, then Safari Back.
- Full suite: 2,792 passed, plus the 1 known `searchable-select` baseline failure.
- After deploy: health OK at 3d72706. The live bundle has the species node (23) with part links and the Parts node (17) with `returnLink`.
- No GROK or AGY UI test ran. The owner gave the deploy go on the evidence above.

### Key Technical Insight
- **A path node must register the same href the link was pending under.** The Parts page used pathname only. The incoming `#diagram-part` link therefore never matched, and the page silently started a new, parentless path.
- **A word appearing is not the word meaning the part**, as in "behavior mirrors the…". Mirrors are linked only on gull-group pages.

## 18:51 EDT - Birds of the World link for subscribers (td-9308e2)

### Summary
Settings has a new "Birds of the World" card with an "I subscribe to Birds of the World" checkbox. When it is ticked, species pages show a "Birds of the World ↗" link under Learn more, right after the eBird link. Deployed as 6d43490.

### What Was Done

**Owner decision:** a checkbox in Settings, with no stored login. The ticket said "add account login info to settings", but the evidence made stored credentials pointless:
- Birds of the World signs in through the same Cornell CAS as eBird (`secure.birds.cornell.edu/cassso`). It uses the same Cornell Lab account.
- Sign-in happens in the user's own browser. The app cannot pass a stored login to it.

**Build**
- `users.bow_subscriber` (migration 0088) is personal to the signed-in account, never its data owner. Viewers can't reach Settings, so they can't set it.
- `birdsOfTheWorldUrl()` in `src/lib/species-links.ts` builds `birdsoftheworld.org/bow/species/{code}/cur/introduction`.
- The link shows for category `species` only:
  - Birds of the World 404s every other category (form, hybrid, intergrade, issf, slash, spuh, domestic), checked live.
  - 30 of 30 random species codes returned 200.
- Help (Settings list) and About (Version History) are updated.

**Verification**
- 9 new tests pass:
  - unit tests for the URL rule;
  - `birds_test` integration tests for the save action and both loaders: off by default, on for the saving account only (not another user, not its viewer), off again, and 409/400/401 cases;
  - `fetch` stubbed to fail, so no provider traffic.
- Full suite: 2,799 passed, plus the 1 known `searchable-select` baseline failure.
- `npm run check` 0/0, build OK.
- Playwright Chromium: 127/127 across 5 themes at 320, 390 and 1200px. It covered the JS and no-JS save, no link on a hybrid's page, contrast ≥7 and 48px targets.
- WebKit: the species checks passed. The owner skipped the Settings pass (see the insight below).
- No CODEX review: the owner ruled it unnecessary for this small change.
- After deploy:
  - migration 0088 applied;
  - health OK at 6d43490;
  - the live bundle's settings node (19) has the checkbox, and its species node (23) has the link.

### Key Technical Insight
- **Headless Playwright WebKit freezes `/settings`** about 2 s after load, which is when the push-subscription `onMount` (`serviceWorker.ready` / `getSubscription`) runs. After that, every evaluate or locator call hangs at 0% CPU, with no timeout.
  - This predates td-9308e2: it reproduced at HEAD with the change stashed.
  - A context with `serviceWorkers: 'block'` avoids it.
  - Two runs hung for over an hour before this was found. Bound browser probes with your own timeouts.
- **This is a test-harness artifact, not an app bug.** Real Safari was not tested.
