## 13:55 EDT - An auk in Parts of a Bird (td-5076e6, epic td-56a506)

### Summary
td-af7c6b (the cormorant) shipped at 3b2382e on 2026-10-19, and the owner approved it and the heron. The owner asked for the auk next. Parts of a Bird now has an eighth drawing, an adult Common Murre, and auk species pages link their part names to it. Built and reviewed; not yet committed: waiting for the owner's go.

### What Was Done

**Prod measurement** (read-only)
- Auk life-list rows: Common Murre, Pigeon Guillemot, Tufted Puffin, Rhinoceros and Cassin's Auklets, and Marbled and Ancient Murrelets, one each. Viewed: Ancient Murrelet, Dovekie, Great Auk, Least and Parakeet Auklets.
- Mostly Pacific birds. The Common Murre is on a life list and lives in both oceans (the Common Guillemot of Norway and Britain), so it is the model.

**Drawings** (`scripts/bird-parts/auk.py`, `auk_flight.py`)
- Traced from openly licensed Commons photos, used for proportions only:
  - standing: "Common Guillemot - Farne Is - FJ0A4774", Francesco Veronesi, CC BY-SA 2.0 (the dark brown British subspecies);
  - flight: "Common murre - Oregon, 2026-5" and "Common murre - Oregon, 2026", Casey Helton, CC BY-SA 4.0. The dark feet show beside the short tail from below.
- Portrait plate (700×1120), like the raptor and the heron. The flank streaks and the toes inside each web are marks, never tap targets.
- Regenerating every bird leaves the other seven drawings' JSON byte-identical.

**Text** (`src/lib/bird-parts/auk.ts`)
- No new part ids: the auk draws crown, nape, face, throat, bill, tarsus, toes, the wing tracts, breast, flanks, belly, undertail coverts and tail.
- The text compares the Thick-billed Murre (bill, gape stripe, breast point, winter face, no bridled form), the Razorbill (bill, eye line, tail), puffins (bill plates, the Atlantic Puffin's rosette), the Rhinoceros Auklet's horn, the Black and Pigeon Guillemots (wing patch, underwing) and foot colours.
- Facts from Wikipedia and the Audubon Field Guide. Not claimed for want of a source in hand: legs set far back, three toes, the white trailing edge of the secondaries, and a Common Murre's underwing colour.

**Term links** (`terms.ts`)
- Alcidae uses the auk drawing. No new terms: the snapshot's 102 auk text rows have no auk-specific technical word, and "bridled" also names a tern.

**Review** (CODEX1, high effort)
- Two P2s and one P3, all fixed:
  - the standing wing's frame-based rows collapsed into slivers and the leftovers were relabelled as tertials. The wing is now split by explicit bands, and the script stops if any wing part comes out in more than one piece;
  - the foot colours are scoped to breeding adults, with the seasonal fading and the young Atlantic Puffin's dark feet;
  - "most auks steer with their feet" narrowed to puffins and the Black Guillemot.
- Re-review clean.

## 14:40 EDT - A tubenose in Parts of a Bird (td-315613, epic td-56a506)

### Summary
td-5076e6 (the auk) shipped at da0d15f, and the owner approved it. The owner asked for the tubenose next. Parts of a Bird now has a ninth drawing, an adult Sooty Shearwater on the water with a close-up of its bill, and species pages for albatrosses, shearwaters, petrels, fulmars and storm-petrels link to it. Built and reviewed; not yet committed: waiting for the owner's go.

### What Was Done

**Model:** a Sooty Shearwater. It is on a production life list (with Pink-footed Shearwater, Northern Fulmar, Black-footed Albatross and two storm-petrels), lives in both the Pacific and the Atlantic, and its silvery underwing stripe is the classic shearwater underwing mark.

**Drawings** (`scripts/bird-parts/tubenose.py`, `tubenose_flight.py`)
- Traced from openly licensed Commons photos, used for proportions only:
  - swimming: "Sooty shearwater kaikoura.jpg", Sabine's Sunbird, CC BY-SA 3.0. It is tilted, so the tracing is rotated about 11° to level the waterline, then scaled and mirrored; parts are clipped at the waterline;
  - bill close-up: "Puffinus griseus -near Avila Beach, California, USA -upper body-8a.jpg", marlin harms, CC BY 2.0;
  - flight: "Puffinus griseus in flight - SE Tasmania (cropped).jpg", JJ Harrison, CC BY-SA 3.0.
- A "Bill, close up" inset draws the bill, nostril tubes and hooked tip a second time, larger. Each is one part, so a tap on the bird or in the inset selects it.
- From below, the silvery underwing coverts carry a dark leading-edge mark (never a tap target).
- Regenerating every bird leaves the other eight drawings' JSON byte-identical.

**Text** (`src/lib/bird-parts/tubenose.ts`)
- New parts: nostril tubes (naricorns) and the hooked tip (maxillary unguis). A tubenose's bill is made of seven to nine horny plates.
- Compares Short-tailed, Pink-footed, Great, Manx and Balearic Shearwaters, the Northern Fulmar, the Black-footed Albatross, and Wilson's and Leach's Storm-Petrels (bill, head pattern, underwing, rump, tail).
- Facts from Wikipedia (Sooty shearwater, Procellariiformes, Albatross, Northern fulmar) and the Audubon Field Guide.

**Term links** (`terms.ts`)
- Diomedeidae, Procellariidae, Hydrobatidae and Oceanitidae use the tubenose drawing. Penguins keep the default.
- New term: naricorns, nasal tubes, tubular nostrils and tube-nose(d), only on tubenose pages. A count over the snapshot's 380 tubenose text rows found them; "nasal" alone is a call elsewhere, and the group noun "tubenoses" stays unlinked.

**Review** (CODEX1, high effort)
- One P2, fixed: the flanks also covered a strip of the forehead and a sliver under the bill, so selecting Flanks outlined part of the head. The head boundaries now overlap, and the script stops if any head, body or wing part comes out in more than one piece.
- Re-review clean.

**Verification**
- `npm run check` 0/0, build OK, `git diff --check` clean.
- Full suite: 2,937 passed, plus the 1 known `searchable-select` failure. The bird-parts tests (109) were re-run after the review fix.
- Playwright, Chromium and WebKit, 5 themes at 320, 390 and 1200 px (30 runs, re-run on the final geometry):
  - a cold `#tubenose-axillaries` opens Flight, below with the axillaries selected;
  - tapping the face on the drawing selects it;
  - the feather layer adds the covert rows; Underwing coverts picked from the list in Flight, below;
  - no button under 48 px, no overflow, minimum contrast 7.79, fixture users deleted.
- iOS Simulator: `#tubenose-nostril-tubes` opens with the tubes highlighted on the bird and in the close-up; taps in the close-up select the hooked tip, the bill and the nostril tubes; from below, taps select the underwing coverts (whole bird and One wing, larger). Fixture user deleted.

### Key Technical Insight
- **A remainder part soaks up every gap.** Using the whole silhouette as the last region (flanks) quietly hands it any sliver the other parts miss, even far away on the forehead. Check that each part is one piece after partitioning.
